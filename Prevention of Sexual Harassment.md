# Prevention of Sexual Harassment

## 1. What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment can be any unwanted behaviour of a sexual nature that makes someone uncomfortable, unsafe, or embarrassed at the workplace. It is not limited to physical contact.

Some examples are:

- Unwanted touching, physical contact, or blocking someone's way.
- Sexual jokes, comments, gestures, or inappropriate conversations.
- Repeatedly asking someone for dates or making unwanted personal comments about their appearance.
- Sending or showing sexual messages, pictures, videos, or other inappropriate content.
- Making sexual advances or asking for sexual favours.
- Making someone feel pressured by connecting work opportunities, promotions, or other benefits with sexual behaviour.
- Repeated behaviour that creates an uncomfortable or hostile work environment.

Even if someone says it was only a joke, it can still be a problem if the behaviour is unwanted and makes the other person uncomfortable.

## 2. What would you do in case you face or witness any incident or repeated incidents of such behaviour?

If I experienced something like this, I would first make it clear, if I felt safe doing so, that the behaviour is not acceptable and should stop. I would also keep a record of what happened, including dates, messages, emails, or other relevant details.

I would report the incident through the appropriate workplace channel, such as the Internal Committee or the person/team responsible for handling such complaints. If I witnessed it happening to someone else, I would support the person, avoid spreading the matter as gossip, and encourage them to report it. If necessary, I would also report what I personally witnessed through the proper channel.

The important thing is not to ignore repeated or serious behaviour. Everyone should be able to work in an environment where they feel safe and respected.
