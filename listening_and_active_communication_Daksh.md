# Listening and Active Communication – Answers

## Question 1
**What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**

- Give the person your full attention.
- Avoid checking your phone or doing other work while listening.
- Don't interrupt while the other person is speaking.
- Maintain eye contact and show that you are listening.
- Try to understand what the person is actually saying.
- Ask questions if something is not clear.
- Repeat or summarize the important points to make sure you understood them correctly.
- Pay attention to their tone and body language.
- Don't start thinking about your reply before they finish speaking.

## Question 2
**According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?**

- Listen carefully to what the other person is saying.
- Try to understand their feelings along with their words.
- Repeat the main idea in your own words.
- Check with the person if you understood them correctly.
- Pay attention to their body language and emotions.
- Clear up any misunderstanding instead of making assumptions.
- Make sure both people have the same understanding of the conversation.

## Question 3
**What are the obstacles in your listening process?**

- Sometimes I get distracted by my phone or other things around me.
- I may start thinking about my response while the other person is still talking.
- If the topic is not interesting to me, I sometimes lose focus.
- Stress or tiredness can also affect my listening.
- Sometimes I assume I already know what the person is going to say.
- I may focus more on the words and miss the person's tone or emotions.

## Question 4
**What can you do to improve your listening?**

- Keep my phone away during important conversations.
- Give the speaker my full attention.
- Let them finish before responding.
- Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
- Take notes during important meetings or technical discussions.
- Repeat the important points to confirm my understanding.
- Try to understand the other person's point of view.
- Stay patient even when I disagree with someone.

## Question 5
**When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I sometimes become passive when I don't want to create an argument or upset someone. For example, if I disagree with a friend or senior, I may stay quiet instead of clearly saying what I think. I also do this when I am not confident about my opinion.

## Question 6
**When do you switch into Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I can become aggressive when I am very frustrated, angry or feel that someone is not listening to me. Sometimes I may speak in a rude or impatient way instead of calmly explaining my point.

## Question 7
**When do you switch into Passive Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?**

This can happen when I am annoyed with someone but don't want to directly talk about the problem. I might use sarcasm, give short replies, ignore the person for some time or make a taunt instead of saying what is actually bothering me.

## Question 8
**How can you make your communication assertive?**

- Say what I think clearly but respectfully.
- Talk about the problem instead of attacking the person.
- Use words like “I feel” or “I think” instead of blaming others.
- Listen to the other person's side as well.
- Say no when I genuinely cannot do something.
- Stay calm even when the other person disagrees.
- Avoid shouting, sarcasm and personal comments.
- Be confident about my opinion while still respecting the other person.
- Try to find a solution instead of just proving that I am right.

## My Takeaway

Good communication is not only about speaking well. It is also about listening properly. I should try to understand people before replying and avoid reacting immediately when I am angry. Being assertive means I can express my thoughts honestly without being rude or disrespectful.
