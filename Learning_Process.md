# Learning Process

## 1. How to Learn Faster with the Feynman Technique

### Question 1: What is the Feynman Technique?

The Feynman Technique is a learning method where you understand a topic by explaining it in simple words as if you are teaching it to someone else.

## 2. Learning How to Learn TED Talk by Barbara Oakley

### Question 2: In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

The most interesting idea for me was that **taking a break from a difficult problem can actually help us solve it**. We often think that we need to continuously struggle with a problem, but sometimes stepping away, relaxing, walking, or doing something else allows our brain to look at the problem differently and make new connections.

I found this interesting because it shows that learning is not only about sitting in front of a book or laptop for many hours. **Focused effort is important, but giving the brain time to relax is also part of the learning process.**

### Question 3: What are active and diffused modes of thinking?

#### Active/Focused Mode

Focused mode is when we concentrate deeply on a specific problem or task.

For example:

> I am trying to understand how `HashMap` works in Java, and I am fully focused on its keys, values, and internal logic.

In this mode, our brain follows familiar patterns and works step by step to solve a problem.

#### Diffused Mode

Diffused mode is when our mind is relaxed and not concentrating directly on the problem.

For example, you may be:

- Walking
- Taking a shower
- Exercising
- Resting
- Doing some other simple activity

During this time, your brain can make connections between different ideas. Sometimes, the solution to a problem suddenly comes to you when you stop actively thinking about it.

**In simple words:** Focused mode helps us work deeply on a problem, while diffused mode helps us see the problem from a broader perspective and make new connections.

## 3. Learn Anything in 20 Hours

### Question 4: According to the video, what are the steps to take when approaching a new topic?

1. **Deconstruct the skill** – Break the topic into smaller and manageable parts.
2. **Learn enough to self-correct** – Understand the basics so that you can recognize and correct your mistakes.
3. **Remove barriers to practice** – Eliminate distractions and anything that prevents you from practicing.
4. **Practice the most important parts** – Spend focused time actually practicing the skill.

# Learning Principles in the Bootcamp

## Question 5: What are some of the actions you can take going forward to improve your learning process?

Going forward, I can take the following actions to improve my learning process:

1. **Focus on understanding instead of just completing topics.** I will try to understand why a concept exists, where it is used, and how it works.

2. **Use the Feynman Technique.** After learning a concept, I will explain it in my own simple words. If I cannot explain it clearly, I will know that I need to understand it better.

3. **Practice concepts instead of only reading or watching videos.** For programming, I will write code, make mistakes, debug them, and solve problems because actual learning happens through practice.

4. **Break large topics into smaller parts.** Instead of saying, *"I need to learn Java Collections,"* I can divide it into smaller topics such as `List`, `Set`, `Queue`, `Map`, iteration, and common use cases.

5. **Give my full attention while studying.** I will reduce distractions by keeping my phone away or on silent and focusing completely on one task at a time.

6. **Take useful breaks.** If I get stuck on a problem for too long, I will take a short break and come back with a fresh mind instead of endlessly staring at the same problem.

7. **Ask questions and stay curious.** Rather than memorizing definitions, I will ask questions such as:
   - Why do we need this concept?
   - Where is it used?
   - What problem does it solve?
   - How can I implement it?

8. **Set deadlines for practice and assignments.** Deadlines will help me manage my time, but my main goal will be to genuinely understand the concept rather than just finish it quickly.

9. **Communicate when I am stuck.** If I face an obstacle that I cannot solve after making a genuine attempt, I will clearly communicate what I tried, where I got stuck, and what I need help with.

10. **Learn concepts deeply enough to teach them.** My goal should not be just *"I have completed this topic."* I should be able to explain it simply, apply it in code, and solve problems using it.

## My Main Takeaway

**I want to focus less on rushing through topics and more on truly understanding them. If I can explain a concept in my own words, implement it myself, and solve problems using it, then I can say that I have actually learned it.**

The most important change I can make is to stay **consistent, curious, focused, and actively involved** in my learning instead of studying passively.
