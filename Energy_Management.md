# Energy Management

## 1. Manage Energy, Not Time

### Question 1: What are the activities you do that make you relax - Calm quadrant?

Some activities that help me relax and feel calm are:

- Listening to music
- Taking a short walk
- Watching something I enjoy
- Talking to friends or family
- Playing games
- Taking a short break from my work or studies
- Sitting quietly and spending some time alone
- Getting enough sleep

### Question 2: When do you find getting into the Stress quadrant?

I usually get into the Stress quadrant when I have too much work to finish in a short time. I also feel stressed when I am learning something difficult, when I am worried about making mistakes, or when I keep thinking about things that I cannot control.

I have noticed that stress is not always bad. Sometimes it pushes me to focus and complete a task. The problem starts when I stay stressed for too long without taking a proper break.

### Question 3: How do you understand if you are in the Excitement quadrant?

I know I am in the Excitement quadrant when I feel energetic, interested, and motivated to do something. I enjoy the task instead of feeling forced to do it. I can focus easily, feel curious about learning more, and usually have a positive mood.

## 2. Understanding Energy Sources

Understanding where my energy comes from helps me manage my day better.

### Physical Energy

- Getting enough sleep and taking proper rest
- Drinking enough water
- Eating healthy and staying hydrated
- Getting some sunlight during the day
- Exercising or doing physical activities
- Taking breaks when my body feels tired

### Emotional Energy

- Spending time on hobbies without worrying about being perfect
- Talking to people I am comfortable with
- Laughing and enjoying simple moments
- Spending quality time with friends and family

### Spiritual Energy

- Helping other people
- Practicing meditation
- Spending some quiet time without distractions
- Being grateful for the things I have

### Intellectual Energy

- Learning something new
- Challenging myself with difficult tasks
- Journaling and thinking about my experiences
- Reflecting on mistakes and learning from them

### Long-Term Practical Steps

- Feeling the stress and completing the task instead of avoiding it
- Maintaining a good sleep routine
- Getting enough physical movement
- Doing light exercise, yoga, or sports
- Practicing meditation
- Following a simple daily routine
- Making time for work, food, sleep, and relaxation
- Spending time with people who are important to me
- Helping others
- Understanding my own habits, emotions, and reactions

### Short-Term Steps

- Taking a short walk
- Doing breathing exercises
- Practicing progressive muscle relaxation
- Using guided imagery
- Taking a few minutes away from the stressful situation
- Counting down from 5 to 1 when I feel angry or overwhelmed

### Fast-Acting Ways

- Talking to or getting a hug from someone I care about
- Taking a bath
- Listening to music
- Doing something related to a hobby
- Giving myself a short break
- Getting a massage or relaxing my body

## 3. Meditation

### Brilliant Things Happen in Calm Minds

A calm mind helps me think more clearly and make better decisions. Meditation can be a simple way to slow down, focus on the present moment, and give my mind a break.

### One Moment Meditation

One moment meditation is simple. I can close my eyes, relax my body, and focus on my breathing for a short time. I do not need to completely stop my thoughts. I just need to notice my breathing and bring my attention back to it whenever my mind wanders.

Even a short meditation can help me pause before continuing with my work.

## 4. Sleep is Your Superpower

### Question 4: Paraphrase the Sleep is Your Superpower video in your own words in brief. Only the points, no explanation.

- Sleep is one of the most important things for our physical and mental health.
- The brain stays active while we sleep and uses this time to recover and organize information.
- Good sleep helps improve memory and learning.
- Lack of sleep can negatively affect concentration, decision-making, and mood.
- Sleep supports the immune system and helps the body recover.
- Regularly getting enough sleep can improve overall health and performance.
- Sleep should be treated as a necessity, not as wasted time.
- Cutting down on sleep to get more work done can actually reduce productivity.
- A good sleep routine helps the body maintain a healthy sleep cycle.
- Prioritizing sleep can make us more energetic and focused during the day.

### Question 5: What are some ideas that you can implement to sleep better?

Some things I can do to improve my sleep are:

- Maintain a fixed sleeping and waking time.
- Avoid using my phone or laptop right before sleeping.
- Avoid heavy meals close to bedtime.
- Keep my room comfortable, quiet, and dark.
- Avoid taking too much caffeine late in the day.
- Exercise regularly, but avoid very intense exercise close to bedtime.
- Create a relaxing bedtime routine.
- Avoid working or studying in bed.
- Try meditation or slow breathing before sleeping.
- Get enough sleep instead of regularly sacrificing sleep for work.

## 5. Brain Changing Benefits of Exercise

### Question 6: Paraphrase the video - Brain Changing Benefits of Exercise. Minimum 5 points, only the points.

- Exercise improves the way the brain functions.
- Physical activity can improve our mood and reduce feelings of stress.
- Exercise helps improve memory and learning.
- Regular exercise can increase the growth and development of brain cells.
- Physical activity improves blood flow to the brain.
- Exercise can help protect the brain as we get older.
- Regular exercise can improve focus and concentration.
- Exercise can help reduce symptoms related to anxiety and depression.
- Being physically active can improve our overall energy levels.
- Exercise does not only benefit the body; it also has a strong impact on the brain.

### Question 7: What are some steps you can take to exercise more?

I can make exercise a regular part of my routine by:

- Starting with small amounts of exercise instead of trying to do too much at once.
- Taking a walk every day.
- Using stairs instead of lifts whenever possible.
- Setting a fixed time for exercise.
- Playing a sport that I actually enjoy.
- Doing simple exercises at home when I cannot go outside.
- Taking short movement breaks during long study or work sessions.
- Exercising with friends to make it more enjoyable.
- Slowly increasing the duration and intensity of my workouts.
- Tracking my progress so I can stay motivated.
